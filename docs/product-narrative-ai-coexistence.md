# Product Narrative — AI Coexistence Visibility

**Updated**: 2026-05-18 JST

## Core Insight

Coexistence Console should not look like a tool that only notices posts that openly say "I used ChatGPT."

The stronger story is that AI-assisted participation will often be blended into normal human conversation. Some contributors will disclose it, some will not, and many posts will be ambiguous. The moderator's problem is not proving who or what wrote a post. The problem is understanding how human, AI-assisted, and unknown participation are shaping the community.

## Product Position

Coexistence Console is not an AI detector.

It is a participation-visibility and governance layer for Reddit moderators. It helps moderators see and manage the coexistence of:

- self-disclosed human-written posts
- self-disclosed AI-assisted posts
- self-disclosed AI-generated posts
- posts with unknown disclosure status
- posts that show explainable review signals such as repetition, low context, or similarity to recent posts

## Future Moderator Thesis

Catch line:

> Useful for today's human moderators, and for tomorrow's AI moderators too.

This is the deeper story behind the product. Reddit moderation is human-led today, but some moderation work may eventually be assisted by or delegated to AI systems. Those future systems should not inherit opaque vibes, brittle detection scores, or unexplained punishments. They will need the same governance rails humans need now:

- clear community policy
- disclosure norms
- explainable queue reasons
- human-reviewable action history
- transparent analytics
- reusable decision context

Coexistence Console is therefore not only a tool for the current AI transition. It is infrastructure for a world where humans, AI participants, human moderators, and eventually AI-assisted moderators all need shared rules and auditable context.

## Why The Distinction Matters

Distinguishing participation modes is useful even when it is uncertain:

- It helps moderators understand whether AI-assisted participation is becoming common.
- It makes human-AI interaction patterns visible without accusing users.
- It supports disclosure culture instead of forcing a ban-or-ignore choice.
- It lets moderators preserve useful AI-assisted posts while reviewing low-context or repetitive content.
- It gives communities a transparent way to govern AI participation as a normal part of Reddit life.

## Safety Boundary

The product must never claim:

- "AI detected"
- "confirmed AI"
- "fake human"
- "bot detected"
- "ban recommended"

Instead, use:

- "unknown disclosure"
- "possible AI-assisted participation"
- "review signal"
- "needs disclosure"
- "needs label"
- "policy review"
- "participation visibility"

## Demo Implication

The strongest demo should not only use a self-disclosed post such as "I used ChatGPT."

It should show two moments:

1. A self-disclosed AI-assisted post gets a transparency label and stays in the community.
2. A non-self-disclosed or ambiguous post is not called AI, but is surfaced as unknown disclosure / review-needed under the community policy.

This proves the product is about governance and visibility, not brittle AI detection.

## Devpost Thesis

AI moderation is not just a detection problem. It is a coexistence visibility problem.

As AI-assisted participation becomes normal, Reddit moderators need to understand how human, AI-assisted, AI-generated, and unknown-disclosure posts interact in their communities. Coexistence Console turns that uncertainty into clear policies, explainable review queues, transparent moderator actions, and ActionLog-based analytics while keeping humans in control.

Short version for Devpost:

> Coexistence Console gives today's human moderators, and tomorrow's AI-assisted moderators, the policy, disclosure, review, and audit rails needed to govern AI-era communities without pretending to perfectly detect AI.
